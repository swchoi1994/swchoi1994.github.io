On November 6, 2026, Amazon began letting customers text its AI shopping agent, Alexa for Shopping. You send a message the way you would to a friend, such as "I'm out of dog food", and the agent searches, suggests products, builds the cart and can place the order. It is the clearest example so far of an AI agent that spends real money from a chat thread, so it is worth understanding before the invitation arrives.

1. What was announced

  • A new way in. Alexa for Shopping already works in the Amazon Shopping app and on the website. Text messaging is a third way to reach the same agent.
  • Invitation only, for now. A small group of US customers received a text inviting them. Amazon says the option will reach "millions more customers" in the coming weeks.
  • It acts, not just answers. Amazon's own summary is that the agent finds products, builds the cart, makes purchases and sends delivery updates.

2. How it works

The conversation also follows you. Amazon says a request started by text carries over to the app, the website and Alexa devices, and back. The agent remembers what you told it, such as "only show me organic options", and uses your shopping history to tailor suggestions.

4. When does it buy without asking?

This is the question that matters, and Amazon's two pages answer it in pieces.

SituationWhat Amazon's pages say
An ordinary requestThe agent messages back with suggestions. "When you're ready", it adds items to the cart and completes the purchase on your behalf.
A reorderIt "finds it and buys it with your approval".
A standing preferenceYou can tell it to "add items to my cart automatically".
Auto-buy at a target priceYou can say "Buy these headphones when they're 30% off". The agent then checks prices and will alert you, or complete the purchase using your default payment method, when the target is reached.

So there are two modes. In the normal flow you approve a purchase. With auto-buy, you give approval in advance, and the order happens later without a second question, charged to your default payment method.

Read your own words carefully"Let me know when it drops to $75" is a price alert. "Buy it when it drops to $75" is a standing order. In a text thread the difference is a single verb.

5. How to get it

  1. Wait for the invitation text, or sign up for news of the next round at amazon.com/text-alexa-shopping, the page Amazon names in its announcement.
  2. Accept the terms.
  3. Link your phone number to your Amazon account.
  4. Start texting. Amazon says a contact called Alexa Shopping will also carry your order updates and delivery notifications over time.

It is limited to the United States for now.

6. Five things to check before you opt in

These are our suggestions, not Amazon's. They follow from what the feature does.

  1. Your default payment method and address. Auto-buy uses the default. Make sure it is the card and the address you would choose.
  2. Who can use your phone. Anyone who can send texts from your phone can now ask an agent to shop on your account. A screen lock matters more than it did.
  3. Any auto-buy rules you have set. Review them from time to time. A rule you forgot is an order you did not expect.
  4. How to switch it off. Find the opt-out before you need it.
  5. Where the invitation came from. See the warning below.
Expect imitation textsAmazon is inviting people by text message, and a text that says "you're invited, tap here to link your account" is exactly what a scam looks like. Do not follow a link in a message you did not expect. Open the Amazon app or type the address yourself, and link your number there.

7. What the announcement does not say

  • A fee. None is mentioned.
  • Other countries. Only the United States is named.
  • An exact date for everyone. "The coming weeks" is as specific as it gets.
  • Every case where approval is skipped. The pages describe approval for purchases and advance approval through auto-buy. They do not give a complete list of settings, so check them yourself once you have access.

Amazon also gives two usage figures: more than 400 million customers used Alexa for Shopping in the past 12 months, and use of its agent features has grown more than eight times since the start of the year. Those are the company's numbers.
