How to calculate mortgage limits: Check market-value brackets, LTV, and DSR in order

Seongwoo Choi · 2026.11.09 · 4 min read

When buying a home, the actual mortgage limit is determined by applying the market-value bracket cap, LTV, and DSR in order; the lowest amount is closest to the available limit.

In this article

  • 1. First check: Limit caps by market-value bracket
  • 2. Second check: Loan-to-value ratio (LTV)
  • 3. Third check: DSR and the stress interest rate
  • 4. Calculation order checklist
  • 5. Points to note

The first question when buying a home is, “How much can I borrow?” However, a mortgage limit is not determined by a single figure. The cap for the property’s market-value bracket, the loan-to-value ratio (LTV), and the debt service ratio (DSR) are applied in order, and the lowest of these amounts is closest to the actual limit. This article lays out that order.

1. First check: Limit caps by market-value bracket

The Financial Services Commission’s 2026 Household Debt Management Plan lists mortgage limit caps for the Seoul metropolitan area and regulated areas by market-value bracket. For properties valued at 1.5 billion won or less, the cap is 600 million won; for properties over 1.5 billion won and up to 2.5 billion won, it is 400 million won; and for properties over 2.5 billion won, it is 200 million won. This system is the differentiated limit standard that took effect on October 16, 2025 (Financial Services Commission press release ↗). The cap is only the starting point and may be reduced further in the two steps below.

2. Second check: Loan-to-value ratio (LTV)

LTV is the ratio applied to a home’s price to determine the amount that can be borrowed. According to guidance from the Financial Services Commission, the mortgage LTV for people without a home and single-home owners subject to a disposal condition in newly designated regulated areas is set at 40% (Financial Services Commission press release ↗). However, it may vary depending on the condition of the home, the purpose of the loan, and when the area was designated, so the final figure must be confirmed through consultation with a bank.

Hypothetical example: Applying an LTV of 40% to a home valued at 1.2 billion won gives a maximum of 480 million won based on the collateral. Since this is lower than the 600 million won cap, LTV is the stronger restriction in this case.

3. Third check: DSR and the stress interest rate

DSR is the ratio of total annual principal and interest payments to annual income. Financial authorities require a stress interest rate of 3.0 percentage points to be added when calculating mortgages in the Seoul metropolitan area and regulated areas (Financial Services Commission press release ↗). Thus, if the actual applicable interest rate is 4.5%, the calculation rate becomes 7.5%.

Hypothetical example: Assuming annual income of 70 million won, annual principal and interest payments of 6 million won on existing loans, a new loan of 480 million won, and a 30-year equal-principal-and-interest repayment schedule, the annual principal and interest payment on the new loan is approximately 40.27 million won at the stress rate of 7.5%. Adding the existing principal and interest payments gives a DSR of approximately 66%. If the bank’s DSR standard is assumed to be 40%, these conditions would be difficult to meet as they stand.

Under the same conditions, meeting a DSR of 40% would limit the annual principal and interest payment on the new loan to approximately 22 million won. Assuming an interest rate of 7.5% and a 30-year equal-principal-and-interest repayment schedule, the loan principal falls to approximately 262 million won. The figures can change substantially if the interest rate or term changes.

4. Calculation order checklist

① Confirm the market value and bracket. Check the bracket table first for the cap. ② Apply the LTV to calculate the amount based on the collateral. ③ Organize your income and existing principal and interest payments. ④ Calculate the new loan’s principal and interest payments using a calculation rate that includes the stress interest rate. ⑤ Calculate the DSR and compare it with the bank’s standard. ⑥ Treat the lowest of the amounts above as the starting point for the limit.

There was guidance that interim-payment loans are not subject to this limit cap (Financial Services Commission 2026 Household Debt Management Plan ↗). However, the applicable scope and conditions differ by product, so they must be checked before signing a contract.

5. Points to note

The examples in this article use hypothetical figures. They do not guarantee actual loan eligibility or an available amount and are not recommendations of financial products. The grace period for applying phase 2 of DSR to regional mortgages is announced differently by period, so check the latest notice. Before taking out a loan, it is safer to check the latest standards with a bank and through the Financial Supervisory Service’s Financial Consumer Information Portal.
