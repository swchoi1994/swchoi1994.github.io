National Pension retroactive payment application process: From checking payment-exemption periods to installment payments

Seongwoo Choi · 2026.11.11 · 5 min read

If you have periods when you could not pay National Pension contributions, you can consider retroactive payments to have those periods recognized as contribution periods; the system is optional, so consider the cost and your circumstances.

If you have periods when you could not pay National Pension contributions because of unemployment or a business suspension, you can consider the retroactive payment system (retroactive payments). Retroactive payments allow you to pay later for periods you could not pay in the past so they can be recognized as contribution periods. According to guidance from the National Pension Service, this is not mandatory, so you can decide whether to apply after considering both the amount and your living circumstances. This article organizes the application process based on the National Pension Service's official guidance.

1. Check the periods eligible for retroactive payment first

According to the National Pension Service's guidance, there are three types of periods eligible for retroactive payment. First are payment-exemption periods when you could not pay contributions because of a business suspension or unemployment. Second are some periods that were excluded from coverage after at least one month of contributions had been paid. For example, these include periods excluded from coverage as a basic livelihood security recipient after April 1, 2001, and working periods excluded from coverage as a workplace subscriber under age 18 after July 29, 2015. Third are periods of military service after January 1, 1988. However, periods included in another public pension, such as the Military Personnel Pension, are excluded, and applications are limited to a maximum of 119 months, including military service.

2. Prepare your eligibility and documents first

You may apply only while maintaining National Pension eligibility. You cannot apply after losing eligibility. The common required document is a marriage certificate (detailed version, with the full resident registration number shown). When applying online or by mobile, you must attach a file of this document for processing. If you divorced or were widowed before 2008, you must also submit a certified copy of the family register. If you apply for a military service period, you need a military service certificate or a copy of your resident registration record containing military service details.

Application methods include in-person visits, mail, fax, Government24, the National Pension Service website, a mobile app, and digital ARS. Submitting the application does not complete the process immediately. The person in charge at the relevant branch office must review the details before processing is completed, and the National Pension Service advises that the periods eligible for application may change before then.

3. How the amount is calculated

The National Pension Service's calculation has two steps. First, it calculates the monthly pension contribution by multiplying the standard monthly income by the contribution rate for the month containing the payment due date, using the month in which the application date falls. Multiplying this by the number of months to be paid retroactively gives the retroactive contribution amount. Voluntary subscribers are subject to a ceiling based on the A value. The A value for 2026 is listed as 3,193,511 won per month, and the A value may change each year.

Let us calculate an example. Assume a standard monthly income of 3 million won, a contribution rate of 9%, and 24 months of retroactive payments. The monthly contribution is 270,000 won (3 million won × 9%), and multiplying by 24 months gives 6.48 million won. These figures are assumptions for explanation and are not the actual application amount. For the actual amount, the National Pension Service must confirm the standard monthly income and contribution rate applicable at the application date and the payment due date.

You can pay the amount in full or in up to 60 monthly installments. If you pay in installments, installment interest calculated using the one-year fixed-term deposit interest rate is added. The notice is sent around the 11th to the 15th of the month following the application month, and payment is due by the end of that month. The National Pension Service advises that if you fail to pay, you will receive notice of one missed payment and no collection action will be taken.

4. Checklist before applying

1) Confirm with the National Pension Service whether your payment-exemption or coverage-exclusion periods are eligible for retroactive payment.

2) Confirm that you currently maintain National Pension eligibility. You must be reporting income or be a voluntary (continuing) subscriber.

3) Prepare files of your marriage certificate and supporting documents for the relevant periods.

4) Decide the number of installments and the amount you can pay each month, then apply.

5) After the branch office review, pay by the deadline when the notice arrives and confirm that the contribution period has been reflected.

5. Points to note before applying

Retroactive payment is an option for increasing your contribution period. How much your future pension will change depends on your individual contribution history and the time you begin receiving benefits, so check with the National Pension Service. The standard monthly income, contribution rate, and A value may change each year, so you should check the latest standards immediately before applying. If money is tight, calculate the installment period and monthly payment amount first. For questions, you can contact the National Pension Service customer center at 1355.
