How to choose permissions when sharing a Google Calendar: distinguishing free/busy visibility from editing access

Seongwoo Choi · 2026.11.07 · 3 min read

When sharing a Google Calendar with family or colleagues, the first thing to decide is whether they can view event details or edit events directly. In short, choose each person’s permission in the sharing settings, then confirm that the invitation email was delivered to them. The steps below are based on Google Calendar’s official help and were not personally tested.

1. Find the calendar to share

On a computer, open calendar.google.com and find the calendar to share under My calendars on the left. To share a calendar owned by someone else, you must have Make changes and manage sharing permission for that calendar. Without this permission, you cannot use the sharing menu.

2. Add people to share with

Click More settings and sharing on the right side of the calendar, then choose Add people and groups under Shared with on the left. Enter the person’s email address or Google Group address. If it is a company or school account, the administrator may have restricted external sharing.

3. Choose a permission: the three most confusing options

First, See only free/busy shows only when someone is busy; event names, locations, and descriptions are not visible. This is the right permission when you want to hide personal events and share only your availability.

Second, See event details shows event names, times, locations, and descriptions. Use it when sharing events that others should be able to view together. It allows viewing only, not editing.

Third, editing access has two levels. Make changes, see private events as free/busy does not allow someone to edit private events. Make changes and see event details lets someone edit events and see who the calendar is shared with. The strongest option, Make changes and manage sharing, also allows someone to share the calendar with others, so grant it only to people who absolutely need it.

4. Confirm that the invitation email was delivered

After choosing a permission, click Send to email the person. They must click the link in the email to add the calendar before they can see the shared events. If the email does not appear, check the address spelling, spam folder, and Trash. If it is still missing, the official help recommends removing the person from the sharing list and adding them again.

5. Do not confuse this with public access

If you set the calendar to public under Access permissions for events on the same settings screen, anyone can find the calendar, unlike with an email invitation. It may appear in internet search results, so turn off public access when you want to show the calendar only to family or colleagues. Pay particular attention to this setting if the calendar contains many personal events.
