How to calculate residential electricity rate tiers: dividing by summer July–August standards

Seongwoo Choi · 2026.11.09 · 3 min read

Residential electricity rates use progressive pricing by consumption tier. This guide explains how to identify the applicable seasonal tiers, calculate energy charges by tier, and separate the remaining bill items.

  • 1. First check the standard-season tier criteria
  • 2. Check the summer tiers separately
  • 3. Calculate 350 kWh using both standards
  • 4. Check items outside the tiers separately
  • 5. Bill checklist

The most confusing part of an electricity bill is why the charges appear different from month to month even when usage is similar. Residential electricity rates are calculated using progressive pricing, with different unit prices for each consumption tier. This article outlines how to check the tier criteria, calculate the energy charge for each tier, and then separate the remaining items on the bill.

1. First check the standard-season tier criteria

The residential progressive-rate table on Korea Electric Power (KEPCO)'s Cyber Branch divides the standard season into 200 kWh or less, 201–400 kWh, and more than 400 kWh. The basic charges are 910 won, 1,600 won, and 7,300 won by tier. The energy-charge unit prices are 120.0 won for the first 200 kWh, 214.6 won for the next 200 kWh, and 307.3 won for the portion exceeding 400 kWh. Apartment complexes and other multi-unit housing may use a different contract structure, so first check the contract type on the bill or with the management office.

2. Check the summer tiers separately

In the same table, the summer standard is divided into 300 kWh or less, 301–450 kWh, and more than 450 kWh. The basic charges are 910 won, 1,600 won, and 7,300 won, and the unit prices by tier are the same as in the standard season. What changes is the tier boundary. Therefore, in summer, the range to which the 120.0-won rate applies extends up to 300 kWh. First check whether the billing period on the bill falls under the summer standard.

3. Calculate 350 kWh using both standards

The figures below are a hypothetical example based on the numbers in the published table, not an actual bill. In the standard season, the first 200 kWh costs 24,000 won (200 × 120.0 won), and the next 150 kWh costs 32,190 won (150 × 214.6 won), making the energy charge 56,190 won. Adding the 1,600-won basic charge gives 57,790 won.

If the same 350 kWh is calculated using the summer standard, the first 300 kWh costs 36,000 won (300 × 120.0 won), and the remaining 50 kWh costs 10,730 won (50 × 214.6 won), making the energy charge 46,730 won. Adding the 1,600-won basic charge gives 48,330 won. Even with the same consumption, the difference in the tier criteria creates a 9,460-won difference in these two items alone.

4. Check items outside the tiers separately

The totals above exclude the climate and environmental charge, fuel-cost adjustment charge, value-added tax, and Electricity Industry Infrastructure Fund. The fuel-cost adjustment charge changes each quarter according to fuel-price fluctuations. Yonhap News Agency reported on March 23, 2026, that the fuel-cost adjustment unit price is set within a range of ±5 won per kWh and that +5 won would remain in effect in the second quarter. Therefore, check the unit price for the quarter applied to the bill on KEPCO ON, and do not simply copy the amount from an old report.

5. Bill checklist

① Write down the consumption (kWh) and billing period shown on the bill. ② Determine whether the standard-season criteria (200/400 kWh) or summer criteria (300/450 kWh) apply. ③ Multiply the consumption in each tier by its unit price and add the basic charge. ④ Match the climate and environmental charge, fuel-cost adjustment charge, value-added tax, and Electricity Industry Infrastructure Fund with the corresponding items on the bill, one by one. ⑤ If the total differs from the bill, check the contract type and whether any discount was applied.

This article presents a calculation method based on the published rate table and news reports. It does not determine the actual bill for a specific household or whether it qualifies for a discount.
