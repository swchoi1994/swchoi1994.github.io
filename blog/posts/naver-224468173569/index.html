Choosing a Claude plan: How to compare Free, Pro, and Max by usage limits

Seongwoo Choi · 2026.11.07 · 4 min read

For personal Claude use, start with Free, move to Pro if you frequently hit session limits, and consider Max for repeated, all-day long tasks; the stated prices and limits were checked on November 7, 2026.

The first thing that confuses personal Claude users is usage limits rather than plan prices. In short, a sensible order is to start with the Free plan to understand your usage pattern, consider Pro if you frequently hit the session limit, and review Max if you repeat long tasks throughout the day. The prices and limits below are based on the official pages checked on November 7, 2026, and may change.

1. Check the price and included features for each plan

According to Anthropic's official pricing page, Free costs $0 and lets you chat on the web, desktop, and mobile, while providing web search and file creation features. Usage is limited. Pro is listed at $17 per month with annual billing ($200 paid upfront annually), or $20 per month with monthly billing, and includes Claude Code and project features. Max costs $100 per month and includes five times more usage than Pro, along with $100 worth of API credits per month. Ultra costs $200 per month and provides 10 times Pro's weekly usage.

The prices are dollar amounts excluding taxes. The amount charged in Korean won varies depending on the checkout screen and tax conditions, so this article does not calculate an exchange rate.

2. Limits are divided into session and weekly limits

Paid plans have two types of usage limits. The first is a session limit that resets every five hours from when the first message is sent, and the second is a weekly limit that resets every week at a set time. Claude Help explains that usage on claude.ai, Claude Code, and the desktop app is combined under the same limit. This is why editing files with Claude Code after having a long conversation on the web can reach the limit faster than expected.

Usage varies depending on conversation length, attached file size, the model used, and the selected task intensity. For that reason, this article does not present a fixed number of messages. Instead, it explains how to decide by checking your own usage rate.

3. Record your usage pattern first

Before choosing a plan, write down how you have used it over the past two weeks in three ways. First, note how many times a day you use it and for how many hours. Second, note whether you upload long documents or code repeatedly. Third, note whether you continuously edit multiple files with Claude Code. The usage screen in Settings > Usage shows your current session usage rate and the next reset time. Checking and recording this screen once or twice a day will show which limit you reach first.

4. Choose a plan step by step

Step 1 is for occasional, short use. If you rarely come close to the limit, Free is sufficient. Step 2 is for cases where you frequently hit the session limit but still have room under the weekly limit. In that case, first compare Pro's greater usage allowance and whether Claude Code is included. Step 3 is for cases where you repeat long tasks for several hours a day and quickly use up Pro as well. Review Max's five times greater usage. Step 4 is for cases where you need to run the longest tasks without worrying about limits. The official page states that Ultra's five-hour limit exemption applies to verified accounts, so check your account conditions first.

The Fable model has separate conditions for each plan. It is listed as being provided through usage credits on Pro and as part of the weekly limit on Max and Ultra, so if you frequently switch models, you should check this separately.

5. What to check before payment

You should check the automatic renewal cycle and the refund and cancellation conditions for annual billing directly on the checkout screen and in the official help documentation. This article did not review the refund conditions. Company accounts and team plans have different administrator features and payment methods, so it is better not to compare them with personal plans in the same table.
