How to calculate when converting a jeonse deposit to monthly rent: Check the conversion cap first

Seongwoo Choi · 2026.11.11 · 4 min read

When you receive a proposal to convert part of a jeonse deposit to monthly rent, the rent is determined by a number called the conversion rate. This article explains how to first check the conversion cap set by the Housing Lease Protection Act and compare the costs of jeonse and monthly rent using the same assumptions. The amounts below are assumptions for demonstrating the calculations and are not actual market prices or the outcome of a specific contract.

1. Calculate the conversion amount first

Assuming that 50 million won of a 300 million won jeonse deposit is converted to monthly rent, the conversion amount is 250 million won (300 million won − 50 million won). Monthly rent is calculated by multiplying this conversion amount by the conversion rate to obtain an annual amount, then dividing it by 12. The contract should first state the conversion amount and whether the entire deposit or only part of it is being converted.

2. Check the legal cap

Article 7-2 of the Housing Lease Protection Act, published by the National Law Information Center, provides that when a deposit is converted into monthly rent, the monthly rent may not exceed the amount obtained by multiplying the converted amount by the lower of two rates. The two rates are (1) the rate prescribed by Presidential Decree and (2) the rate obtained by adding the rate prescribed by Presidential Decree to the Bank of Korea base rate. The Korea Real Estate Board·LH Lease Dispute Mediation Committee’s monthly-rent conversion calculator displays the calculation rate by adding 2.0 percentage points to the base rate, and shows the base rate for the change dated July 16, 2026, as 2.75%. Calculated this way, the rate is 4.75%. However, this does not mean that this is the actual cap for every contract. Because the lower rate must be used after comparing it with the rate prescribed by Presidential Decree in (1), the law and calculator should be checked together at the time of the contract.

3. List monthly rent side by side for each conversion-rate assumption

Using a conversion amount of 250 million won, the figures change as the assumed conversion rate changes. The 4.75% rate is an example of a cap obtained using the calculation method above, while 4.00% and 3.00% are assumptions for explanation.

- 4.75%: 11.875 million won per year, approximately 989,583 won per month

- 4.00%: 10 million won per year, approximately 833,333 won per month

- 3.00%: 7.5 million won per year, 625,000 won per month

Even with the same deposit, monthly rent changes when the assumed conversion rate changes. Therefore, a comparison table must state not only the monthly rent amount but also the assumed conversion rate.

4. Include the cost of keeping the deposit elsewhere

Converting to monthly rent creates a monthly payment but reduces the deposit. Therefore, when comparing the two contracts, the interest that would be earned by keeping the deposit elsewhere, or the interest paid when using a loan—in other words, the opportunity cost—must be included on the same basis. For example, assume an opportunity cost of 3.00% per year. Keeping a jeonse deposit of 300 million won produces an annual cost of 9 million won. Paying a 50 million won deposit and monthly rent of 833,333 won at a 4.00% conversion rate produces an annual opportunity cost of 1.5 million won, to which annual rent of 10 million won is added, for a total of 11.5 million won. Under these assumptions, the jeonse option costs 2.5 million won less per year. Because the result changes when the assumptions change, it is important to state the opportunity-cost assumption first.

5. Pre-contract checklist

1) Confirm that the contract states how much of the deposit is the conversion amount.

2) Check both the statutory wording and the calculator’s reference date to confirm that the monthly rent does not exceed the legal cap.

3) First confirm the means of securing the return of the deposit. The order for reading the register of real estate is summarized in The order for reading the register of real estate before a jeonse contract ↗.

4) List management fees, brokerage fees, and moving costs separately in the comparison table.

5) If you may move out during the contract period, divide the two figures by each period and calculate them separately. If you need an assumption for loan interest, refer to the calculation process in How to calculate the limit of a home mortgage loan ↗.

Limitations of this article

This article explains a calculation method based on the wording of the law and the calculator. It does not address market prices in specific regions, actual contract terms, or interest-rate forecasts. Because the base rate changes, the Bank of Korea’s announcement and the calculator’s reference date at the time of the contract must be checked. For disputes requiring legal interpretation, seek help from a professional counseling organization.
