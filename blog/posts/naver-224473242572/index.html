How to calculate housing subscription points: the order for separately counting the no-homeownership period, dependents, and account tenure

Seongwoo Choi · 2026.11.11 · 5 min read

The housing subscription points system adds scores for the no-homeownership period, number of dependents, and housing subscription account tenure, for a maximum of 84 points. Calculate each category separately, then confirm the result against the property’s official notice.

The housing subscription points system adds scores for three categories: the no-homeownership period, the number of dependents, and the housing subscription account tenure. The maximum score is 84 points. This article organizes the order for calculating the three categories separately based on the official points table. The actual application score is finalized according to the resident recruitment notice for the relevant property.

1. Check the maximum scores for the three categories

According to the points-system criteria in the appendix, the no-homeownership period is worth 32 points, the number of dependents 35 points, and the housing subscription account tenure 17 points. The total is 32 + 35 + 17 = 84 points. Since omitting even one category leads to an incorrect score, it is best to write down the three categories separately before calculating.

2. No-homeownership period: count from the notice date

The no-homeownership period is based on the period during which all household members have owned no home as of the date of the resident recruitment notice. Less than 1 year is 2 points, and at least 1 year but less than 2 years is 4 points, increasing by 2 points for each additional year; at least 7 years but less than 8 years is 16 points, and 15 years or more is 32 points. An unmarried person without a home who has not owned a home before turning 30 is assigned 0 points.

For example, a no-homeownership period of at least 7 years but less than 8 years earns 16 points. If a home was previously sold, the period is counted from the time of sale.

3. Number of dependents: exclude the applicant

Dependents are the people who meet the criteria, such as a spouse, home-free direct ascendants, and unmarried children. The applicant is excluded from the calculation. Zero dependents is 5 points, 1 is 10 points, 2 is 15 points, 3 is 20 points, 4 is 25 points, 5 is 30 points, and 6 or more is 35 points.

Direct ascendants must not own a home or a sales right to a home to be recognized. If even one direct ascendant owns a home, that direct ascendant and their spouse are both excluded from the dependents. There is also a period requirement for being listed on the same resident registration record, so the wording of the official notice must be checked.

4. Housing subscription account tenure: use the original opening date

Account tenure is determined by the subscription period as of the date of the resident recruitment notice. Even if the account type, amount, or name is changed, the original opening date remains the reference date. The score starts at 1 point for less than 6 months and increases as the period grows, with a category maximum of 17 points. At least 10 years but less than 11 years earns 12 points.

In the points-based system for private housing general supply, the spouse’s account tenure may be combined. According to Hana Bank’s information page, 50% of the spouse’s account period may be added, recognized up to a maximum of 3 points, and the combined account score for the applicant and spouse cannot exceed 17 points. To combine the periods, the spouse’s account must be valid as of the notice date and must not be canceled before the contract.

5. Calculate an example household

Assumption: a no-homeownership period of at least 7 years but less than 8 years, 2 dependents, and account tenure of at least 10 years but less than 11 years. The no-homeownership period is 16 points, dependents are 15 points, and the account is 12 points. The total is 16 + 15 + 12 = 43 points.

Even for the same person, the periods may change if the notice date changes. Since a one-day difference can sometimes move the period into a different range, the calculation must be redone using the notice date.

6. Check with the calculator and finalize with the official notice

The Korea Housing and Urban Fund’s Quick Housing Subscription Points Calculator shows the score for each category and the total after the no-homeownership period, number of dependents, and account tenure are entered. 1) Count the no-homeownership period as of the notice date. 2) Count dependents excluding the applicant. 3) Count account tenure from the original opening date. 4) Enter the three values into the calculator and check the total. 5) Check whether it matches the points-calculation criteria in the official notice for the relevant property.

The calculator page states that it is a service for viewing an estimated score and that the applicant is responsible for submitting an incorrect application. Therefore, before applying for housing, the result must be checked again against the official notice.

7. Limitations of this article

This article summarizes the system’s criteria table and does not predict the likelihood of winning a particular property. Whether special supply, newlywed special supply, or the private-housing points system applies differs by property, so the resident recruitment notice must take priority. Not all detailed exceptions are covered here.
