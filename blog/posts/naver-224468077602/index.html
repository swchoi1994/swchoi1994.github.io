How to create and store Google 2-Step Verification backup codes before losing your phone

Seongwoo Choi · 2026.11.07 · 3 min read

Create and securely store Google 2-Step Verification backup codes in advance so you can recover access if you lose your phone, change your number, or cannot receive a code through your usual method.

If you use Google 2-Step Verification, it is a good idea to create backup codes in advance in case you lose the phone that receives authentication codes. Backup codes are recovery codes for signing in when your usual authentication methods are unavailable. You normally will not need them, but they can prevent your account from being locked when you change devices or phone numbers.

1. First, check when backup codes are needed

Google Help explains that backup codes should be used if you lose your phone, change your phone number, or cannot receive codes by text message, phone call, or Google Authenticator. In other words, they are a fallback method for situations in which all your usual sign-in methods are blocked. 2-Step Verification must already be enabled on your account to use backup codes.

2. Create a set of codes under Security and sign-in

Go to your Google Account (myaccount.google.com) and open the “Security and sign-in” menu. Under “How you sign in to Google,” click “2-Step Verification”; you may be asked to confirm your sign-in. Then, under “Backup codes,” click “Continue” to create a set of 10 codes. Menu names may vary slightly depending on the account type or screen version, so look for items with the same meaning.

3. Remember that each code can be used only once

Each backup code is eight digits long and is deactivated after being used for one sign-in. Therefore, it is important to mark the codes you have used when storing the list. If you lose the codes or use them all, you can click “Refresh” on the same screen to create a new set. However, creating a new set automatically makes the previous set unusable, so you can discard the old list after receiving the new set.

4. Store them in a secure place

Google advises printing the codes and storing them in a safe place where you keep important documents, such as your passport. You can also download them as a file to your computer, but storing that file in a shared or synchronized folder creates an exposure risk. Consider keeping one printed copy and another copy in a location with restricted access, such as a note in a password manager. It is best to avoid transcribing the codes into a messenger app or email.

5. Check that your stored codes are still usable

If you simply store the codes and stop there, it can be difficult to verify them when you actually need them. A sign-in test consumes one code, so perform one only when necessary. Instead, make a one-line note of the date you created the new set and where it is stored so you can immediately know which list to use when changing devices. Important notes

Google Help states that accounts using Advanced Protection cannot download recovery codes to a computer. Also, Google will not ask for recovery codes outside the sign-in process, so do not provide them if someone asks you for a code.
