How to read the power-grid impact assessment for AI data centers: Only 59 of 999 applications passed

Seongwoo Choi · 2026.11.10 · 3 min read

Applicants undergo a technical review, receive a supply-availability response from KEPCO, and submit an impact assessment before deliberation; only 59 of 999 applications passed, totaling about 5.9 GW.

In this article

  • 1. Four stages from application to approval
  • 2. What changes after March 2027
  • 3. Points to check when reading

AI data centers use a lot of electricity. Therefore, a system exists to assess in advance how they will affect the surrounding power grid before large power demand arises. This is the power-grid impact assessment. It applies to businesses that use 10 MW or more of electricity and was introduced in 2024 with the enforcement of the Special Act on the Promotion of Distributed Energy. Its purpose is to distribute facilities near power-generation sites rather than concentrating them in the Seoul metropolitan area, where electricity demand is concentrated.

1. Four stages from application to approval

First, the business applies for an initial technical review, stating, 'We will use this much electricity at this location starting on this date.' Second, the Korea Electric Power Corporation reviews the available capacity at nearby substations and transmission and distribution lines and responds on whether supply is possible. Third, after receiving a response that supply is possible, the business submits an assessment report covering the grid impact and burden-mitigation measures such as self-generation and ESS, which is then deliberated by the Electricity Policy Deliberation Committee of the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment. Fourth, only approved projects proceed to the electricity-use application and contract stages.

Over the two years from August 2024 to August 2026, 999 initial technical review applications for data centers were filed, totaling about 82 GW. It should be taken into account that duplicate applications may be included. Of these, KEPCO responded that supply was possible for 577 applications, totaling about 45.6 GW. Among the projects that reached deliberation, 59, totaling about 5.9 GW, passed. Compared with the applied-for capacity, this is about 7%. This means that most applications are screened out before deliberation.

2. What changes after March 2027

When the 'Special Act on the Promotion of the Artificial Intelligence Data Center Industry' takes effect in March 2027, AI data centers of a certain size or smaller built outside the Seoul metropolitan area are expected to be exempt from this assessment. This is a measure intended to move data centers from the metropolitan area to other regions and attract investment more quickly. However, to qualify for an exemption, a facility must be recognized as an AI data center, and the criteria have not yet been established by an enforcement decree.

The exemption criteria are being discussed among government ministries. The Ministry of Science and ICT is reportedly considering an exemption for facilities of 100 MW or less, while the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment is considering 50 MW or less. The newspaper estimated the possibility of exemption by applying the criteria to 340 applications outside the metropolitan area, totaling 38.1 GW. At 100 MW or less, 253 applications, or 74.4%, would qualify; at 50 MW or less, 108 applications, or 31.8%, would qualify. Combining these, it presented a range of 30% to 74% at most. This figure is close to an upper bound based on the assumption that all applications would be recognized as AI data centers.

3. Points to check when reading

First, applications must be distinguished from actual electricity demand. The 999 applications and 82 GW represent the application scale, while the electricity actually connected is the 5.9 GW in projects that passed deliberation. Second, the exemption rate is an estimate. The figure may change until the government finalizes the criteria. Third, power-use efficiency and renewable-energy procurement are separate issues from this assessment. Germany specifies a PUE target in law, while China uses PUE ratings as criteria for administrative review.
