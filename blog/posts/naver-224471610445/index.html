How to check eligibility for the Earned Income Tax Credit: From household type to asset criteria

The Earned Income Tax Credit is a system that supports households that work but have low incomes. Before applying, check household type, income criteria, asset criteria, and exclusion categories in order. The information below is organized based on the National Tax Service guidance on eligibility and application periods. The National Tax Service determines whether payment is actually made.

1. Determine the household type first

The income criteria vary by household type. You are a single-person household if you have no spouse and no dependent child under 18 or lineal ascendant aged 70 or older. You are a single-income household if your spouse's total salary, etc. is less than 3 million won. Even without a spouse, you may be considered a single-income household if you have a dependent child under 18 or a lineal ascendant aged 70 or older, but that person's annual income amount must be 1 million won or less, and they must live with you and share your livelihood at the same registered address. You are a dual-income household if your own and your spouse's total salary, etc. are each at least 3 million won.

2. Income criteria: Check combined total income for the couple

The income requirement is based on whether the couple's combined annual total income is below the threshold for their household type. The Earned Income Tax Credit thresholds are 22 million won for a single-person household, 32 million won for a single-income household, and 44 million won for a dual-income household. Total income includes earned income, business income, religious-person income, other income, and interest, dividend, and pension income; tax-exempt income, retirement income, and capital gains are excluded.

Hypothetical example A: If a single-person household has 20 million won in earned income (total salary) and 3 million won in interest income, the total is 23 million won. Because this exceeds the 22 million won threshold, this example fails the income requirement.

Hypothetical example B: Manufacturing business income is calculated by multiplying revenue by the industry-specific adjustment rate of 40%. If revenue is 50 million won, the calculated business income is 20 million won. This is a hypothetical value intended to show the calculation method. If you have business income, you must apply for the Earned Income Tax Credit during the regular application period.

3. Asset criteria: The total value of assets, not market price

The asset requirement is based on whether the total value of the homes, land, buildings, passenger vehicles, rental deposits, financial assets, and other assets held by all household members as of June 1, 2025, is less than 240 million won. Debt is not deducted from the total assets. If the total is at least 170 million won but less than 240 million won, only 50% of the calculated amount is paid. A home's rental deposit is valued at the lower of the actual deposit or the deemed deposit (standard market value × 55%), so it should not be judged solely from the lease contract.

Income is measured on an annual basis for 2025, while assets are measured as of June 1, 2025. Because the reference dates differ, using data from the same month for both criteria may lead to an incorrect conclusion.

4. Check exclusion categories and the application period

Even if you meet the requirements, you cannot apply if you do not have Korean nationality (with some exceptions), are another resident's dependent child, or operate a professional business. If you are a regular wage worker whose average monthly earned income is at least 5 million won, you cannot apply for the Earned Income Tax Credit.

You must also check the application period. According to the National Tax Service guidance, the regular application period for 2025 income was from May 1 to June 1, 2026, and the late application period runs until December 1, 2026. The semiannual application for the first half of 2026 income (September 1 to 15) has already passed. The semiannual application for the second half of 2026 income is from March 1 to 15, 2027. Applications can be made through Hometax or ARS (1544-9944).

5. Checklist before applying

① Determine the household type: Check whether you have a spouse, dependent children, or a lineal ascendant aged 70 or older. ② Compile income by category: List earned, business, religious-person, other, and interest, dividend, and pension income separately. ③ Compile assets as of June 1, 2025: Apply the deemed rental deposit rules for homes to rental deposits. ④ Check the exclusion categories. ⑤ Check the deadline: Late applications are accepted until December 1, 2026. ⑥ If anything is unclear, first confirm with the Incentive Consultation Center (1566-3636) or the competent tax office. The National Tax Service makes the final determination.
