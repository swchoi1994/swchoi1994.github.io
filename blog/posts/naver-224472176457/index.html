Order for applying for a long-term care grade for older adults: from checking eligibility to the home visit survey and grade criteria

Seongwoo Choi · 2026.11.10 · 5 min read

Long-term care insurance helps older adults who struggle with daily life or have geriatric illnesses access care services; this guide covers eligibility, applications, documents, surveys, and grading based on official guidance checked on November 10, 2026.

In this article

  • 1. Check eligibility first
  • 2. Choose the application method and documents
  • 3. Know what the home visit survey examines
  • 4. Read the grade criteria: score ranges and the Grade Assessment Committee
  • 5. Checklist before applying

Long-term care insurance is a system that enables older adults who have difficulty managing daily life on their own, or people with geriatric illnesses such as dementia and cerebrovascular disease, to receive care services. Preparation is much easier if you separate the eligibility requirements, application method, documents, home visit survey, and grade-assessment process before applying. The information below is compiled from official guidance from the National Health Insurance Service and Easy Law Information, checked on November 10, 2026. Because the grade is determined through the survey results and deliberation, this article cannot be used to predict an individual's grade in advance.

1. Check eligibility first

People who can apply are those aged 65 or older, or those under 65 who have a geriatric illness specified by presidential decree, such as dementia or cerebrovascular disease. Eligible people include long-term care insurance subscribers and their dependents, as well as medical aid beneficiaries. One point to check is whether you are using or wish to use disability activity support benefits. According to the National Health Insurance Service, receiving a long-term care grade restricts applications for disability activity support, and canceling the grade does not make a disability activity support application possible. If this applies to you, it is safest to contact the National Pension Service (1355) before applying.

2. Choose the application method and documents

The applicant or a representative may apply. Representatives include family members, relatives or interested parties, dedicated social welfare officials, and the head of a dementia relief center (when the applicant has dementia), among others. Applications can be submitted by visiting an NHIS branch (operating center), by mail, fax, or online; telephone applications are available only for renewal applications. Online applications are available only to people aged 65 or older or applicants submitting a renewal application while under 65, and require login with a joint certificate. People under 65 making a first application cannot submit it online, so they must apply in person or by mail.

For an in-person application, you must present identification; for an application by mail or fax, a copy of your identification is required. The application form is the Long-Term Care Recognition Application (Form No. 1-2 in the annex), which can be obtained from an NHIS branch or the forms library on the Long-Term Care Insurance website. As a rule, the doctor's opinion must be submitted with the application, but people aged 65 or older may submit it before the Grade Assessment Committee receives the deliberation materials. If a person under 65 does not submit a doctor's opinion, they must attach documentary evidence, such as a medical certificate confirming the geriatric illness, to the application.

3. Know what the home visit survey examines

After you apply, an NHIS employee visits your residence to conduct a survey. The visit schedule is provided in advance, and the place and time can be adjusted in consultation with the employee. The survey form consists of 90 items, of which 65 are used to calculate the recognition score. The areas included in the score are physical function, cognitive function and behavioral changes, nursing care, and rehabilitation. Physical function assesses the level of help received from other people in daily life during the past month, while nursing care assesses whether symptoms have been present during the past two weeks. Therefore, before the survey, making notes with dates about what help was needed during the past month will help you explain the situation accurately.

4. Read the grade criteria: score ranges and the Grade Assessment Committee

The regional Long-Term Care Grade Assessment Committee assesses the survey results and deliberation materials, including the doctor's opinion, according to the criteria. The committee has 15 members, including the chairperson, and includes at least one doctor and at least one Korean medicine doctor. The official recognition-score criteria are as follows: Grade 1 is 95 points or higher; Grade 2 is 75 points or higher and below 95 points; Grade 3 is 60 points or higher and below 75 points; and Grade 4 is 51 points or higher and below 60 points. Grade 5 is for a person with dementia who scores 45 points or higher and below 51 points, while the cognitive support grade is for a person with dementia who scores below 45 points.

The grade is determined based on how much help a person needs from others in daily life. If the score does not meet the criteria, the person may be assessed as outside the grading system, so it is safest not to make a living plan based on a particular grade before applying.

5. Checklist before applying

① Check whether you are aged 65 or older, or, if under 65, whether you have a medical certificate or other document proving a geriatric illness. ② First ask the National Pension Service (1355) whether you are using disability activity support. ③ Prepare your identification, application form, and doctor's opinion or medical certificate. ④ Decide whether to apply in person or online; if applying online, prepare a joint certificate. ⑤ Confirm the survey date and make notes about the help you needed during the past month. ⑥ When you receive the recognition certificate, check the grade, validity period, and service-use plan. If you wish to give up a grade you have already received because of social welfare services under another law, you may submit a Long-Term Care Grade Waiver Application and your identification to the operating center.
