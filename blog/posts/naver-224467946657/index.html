How to check and organize devices signed in to your Google account: Sign out of unknown sessions

Seongwoo Choi · 2026.11.06 · 3 min read

Open your Google Account’s “Your devices” list to identify unfamiliar devices or sessions, verify their details, and sign out only of those you do not recognize.

If you are wondering whether an unfamiliar device is signed in to your Google account, the quickest approach is to first open the account’s “Your devices” list. The list shows devices and sessions that are currently signed in or signed in within the past few weeks. You only need to select and sign out of items you do not use; the others can remain as they are. I have separately explained how to strengthen the sign-in method itself in Differences between passkeys, passwords, and two-step verification ↗.

1. How to open the device list

On a PC browser, open your Google Account settings and select the “Security and sign-in” menu. In the “Your devices” panel, click “Manage all devices” to open the device and session list. Google Help says you can perform the same check at google.com/devices. On a phone, the app menu location may vary, so follow the “Security” or “Devices” item shown on the screen.

2. Three things to read in the list

The first is the device name. The second is the displayed time. According to Google Help, this time is when the device or session last communicated with Google’s systems, so it may appear more recent than the time you actually used the device. Automatic synchronization is included as well. The third is the status. Devices or sessions that have been signed out are marked “Signed out.” If the same device name appears on multiple lines, they may be multiple sessions from the same device, or they may actually be different devices.

3. How to determine whether a device is unfamiliar

Let us use a hypothetical example. Suppose “Unknown Windows PC” appears in the list, and you have never used that PC. The first question is whether you recently used a new PC or signed in on a public computer such as one at a library. If so, the device may reflect your own activity, so check the session details to confirm. The second question is whether you recently reset a PC. A device from before the reset may remain on the list, so think back to any older session that appears to be the same type of device. The third question is whether you remember the displayed location. Google Help explains that a nearby location may be shown instead of the exact location. If it is difficult to answer all three questions, it is safer to sign out of the device.

4. How to sign out and what to watch for

Select the device you want to organize and then choose “Sign out.” If multiple sessions appear under the same name, you should sign out of all sessions with that name before considering access from that device to be cut off. If you remove only one item and leave the others, the same device may still be signed in. After signing out, reopen the list and check whether the item has changed to “Signed out.”

5. What to check afterward

If you signed out of a device you use, make sure you have the password and authentication method needed to sign in again. Signing out from the Google Account list does not by itself erase the browser history on a public PC where you signed in. You must separately check the history and saved sign-in information in that PC’s browser.
