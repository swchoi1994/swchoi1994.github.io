How to Check Pension Savings and IRP Tax Credit Limits Before Year-End Tax Settlement: Distinguishing 6 Million Won and 9 Million Won

Seongwoo Choi · 2026.11.10 · 4 min read

Pension savings and IRP accounts qualify for year-end tax credits, but the amount deposited and the amount eligible for a credit differ. Check the limits, rate, tax due, ISA funds, and payment deadline in order.

  • 1. Distinguish the payment limit from the tax credit limit
  • 2. Check the credit rate by income bracket
  • 3. Check whether your final tax due is sufficient
  • 4. Check ISA maturity funds and the payment deadline
  • 5. Check the tax before making an early withdrawal
  • 6. Checklist before year-end tax settlement

Pension savings and IRP (individual retirement pension) accounts are representative pension accounts eligible for a tax credit during year-end tax settlement. However, the amount deposited and the amount eligible for a tax credit are different. Following the steps below allows you to calculate the eligible amount separately. The information below is organized based on guidance from the National Tax Service and financial explanatory materials. The National Tax Service determines the final eligibility and refund amount.

1. Distinguish the payment limit from the tax credit limit

The amount you can deposit into pension accounts and the amount eligible for a tax credit are separate. If you use only a pension savings account, the annual payment limit eligible for a tax credit is 6 million won. If you use a retirement pension account (such as an IRP) as well, the two accounts combined are eligible for a credit up to 9 million won per year. The total annual payment limit for all pension accounts is 18 million won, but amounts exceeding 9 million won are excluded from the tax credit.

2. Check the credit rate by income bracket

The credit rate is 15% if your comprehensive income is 45 million won or less (or if you have only earned income, if your total salary is 55 million won or less), and 12% if it is higher. Some materials explain the rates as 16.5% or 13.2% after adding local income tax. Regardless of which standard is used, the calculation is the same: multiply the eligible amount by the credit rate.

Example A: An employee with a total salary of 50 million won deposited 9 million won into a pension savings account only. Since the eligible amount is limited to 6 million won for pension savings, the calculated credit is 6 million won × 15% = 900,000 won. The excess 3 million won is not eligible for the credit.

Example B: The same employee deposited 6 million won into pension savings and 3 million won into an IRP. Since the combined 9 million won is eligible, the calculated credit is 9 million won × 15% = 1.35 million won. Even when the same 9 million won is deposited, the eligible amount differs depending on how it is divided between the accounts.

Example C: An employee with a total salary of 60 million won deposited 9 million won into pension accounts. Because the credit rate is 12%, the calculated credit is 9 million won × 12% = 1.08 million won. This value is also an illustrative calculation.

3. Check whether your final tax due is sufficient

A tax credit reduces the calculated tax by the credited amount. If your final tax due is less than the credit, you may not receive the full maximum calculated credit. This is why you need to check the final tax due after considering other tax credits in addition to earned-income tax.

4. Check ISA maturity funds and the payment deadline

The National Tax Service states that if you deposit all or part of the balance into a pension account after the ISA contract period ends, 10% of the transferred amount, up to a maximum of 3 million won, can be added to the tax credit limit. This additional limit applies only in the year when the ISA maturity funds are deposited into a pension account. Also confirm that payments into pension accounts for the applicable tax credit year must be completed by the end of the financial institution's business hours on December 31.

5. Check the tax before making an early withdrawal

Payments that received a tax credit and investment earnings may be subject to other income tax when the account is terminated early or funds are withdrawn in a way other than as a pension. The taxation method differs for principal that did not receive a tax credit. Early withdrawals from an IRP are restricted unless they are for legally recognized reasons. Therefore, filling the tax credit limit should not be the goal, and it is safer not to deposit living expenses needed in the short term into these accounts.

6. Checklist before year-end tax settlement

① Check the total amount deposited into pension savings and the IRP. ② Calculate the eligible amount within the limits of 6 million won for pension savings and 9 million won combined. ③ Use your total salary or comprehensive income to determine whether the applicable bracket is 15% or 12%. ④ Check whether your final tax due can cover the credit amount. ⑤ If this is the year you transferred ISA maturity funds, calculate the additional limit separately (up to 3 million won). ⑥ Check whether the payments were completed before December 31 and whether money needed within three years has been included.
