How to read apartment transaction prices: Check the contract date, exclusive area, and canceled transactions first

Seongwoo Choi · 2026.11.09 · 3 min read

This article explains how to read apartment transactions on the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport’s system by checking contract dates, exclusive area, floors, and transaction status before comparing prices.

In this article

  • 1. Distinguish the contract date from the reflection date
  • 2. Match the exclusive area and floor
  • 3. Check canceled, corrected, and duplicate entries
  • 4. Look at the lowest, median, and highest prices together instead of only the average
  • 5. Checklist of the verification order
  • 6. When the data is confusing

When you see news about housing prices, “the highest price” or “the average price” often catches your eye first. However, the meaning of a single actual transaction varies depending on the contract date, area, floor, and transaction status. This article organizes the order of checks for reading apartment transactions on the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport’s Actual Transaction Price Disclosure System ↗. It is not about deciding whether a particular area will rise or fall, but about separating the items to check before comparing numbers.

1. Distinguish the contract date from the reflection date

First, check whether the date in the public list is the actual contract date or the date the transaction was reflected in the reported transactions list. There may be a time lag between reporting and disclosure, so you should not conclude that market prices have risen or fallen based only on data from the past few weeks. When comparing transactions, it is safer to look at several months or more together.

2. Match the exclusive area and floor

Even within the same complex, 84㎡ and 59㎡ of exclusive area have different price standards. Match the area range first, before calculating the price per pyeong. It is also easier to identify the conditions of the transaction represented by a number in a news headline if you note the floor, building, and construction year.

Here is a calculation example using hypothetical figures. If an 84㎡ exclusive-area unit traded for KRW 1.5 billion, 84㎡ is approximately 25.4 pyeong, so the converted price per pyeong is approximately KRW 59 million. This value is an example intended to show the calculation method and is not an actual transaction record. Compare converted prices per pyeong only among transactions using the same area standard.

3. Check canceled, corrected, and duplicate entries

Check whether any transactions were canceled or had their reported details corrected. The way items are displayed on the search screen may vary depending on the period and menu, so it is a good idea to read the on-screen guidance as well. If the same amount appears twice on the same day, first check whether the building, floor, and area are actually different, and then determine whether the entries are duplicates.

4. Look at the lowest, median, and highest prices together instead of only the average

The average can be strongly affected by a few high-priced transactions. If you record the lowest price, median, and highest price together within the same period and area range, it becomes easier to identify the difference between a news headline and the actual transaction range. In Excel or Google Sheets, you can calculate the median with the MEDIAN function.

5. Checklist of the verification order

① Select the property type and area, such as apartments or row houses/multi-family housing. ② Set the search period and account for the reflection lag in recent data. ③ Match the exclusive-area range. ④ Note differences in floor, building, and construction year. ⑤ Check for canceled, corrected, or duplicate indicators. ⑥ Record the lowest price, median, and highest price together. ⑦ Check the original article to determine what the “average” mentioned in the news actually means.

6. When the data is confusing

If you do not understand the on-screen explanations or list display, you can contact the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport Actual Transaction Price Disclosure System call center (1533-2949, weekdays 09:00–18:00, excluding lunch hours from 12:00–13:00; closed on weekends and public holidays). When downloading data from the official system, saving the search criteria and date together also makes it easier to reproduce the same results later.

This article summarizes the official site’s guidance and general calculation methods. It is not the result of analyzing actual transaction data for a particular complex and does not recommend investing or purchasing.
