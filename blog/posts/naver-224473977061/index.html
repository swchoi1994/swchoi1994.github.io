How to distinguish Credit Counseling and Recovery Committee debt adjustment programs by days past due

Seongwoo Choi · 2026.11.12 · 3 min read

This article explains which Credit Counseling and Recovery Committee debt adjustment program to consider based on days past due and what to prepare before applying; eligibility figures may change, so check the Committee’s notices.

When an account becomes overdue, it can be confusing to know where to seek advice. The Credit Counseling and Recovery Committee (CCRC) operates debt adjustment programs for people who have difficulty repaying debts owed to financial companies. This article organizes which program to consider first based on the number of days past due and what to prepare before applying. Because eligibility figures may change, use the CCRC’s notices for the final determination.

1. The first criterion is the number of days past due

According to guidance from various financial companies and explanations of CCRC programs, debt adjustment is divided by the overdue period. Accounts up to 30 days past due are generally directed to Rapid Debt Adjustment, those 31–89 days past due to Pre-Workout, and those 90 days or more past due to Individual Workout. If you have multiple loans, the number of days past due may differ for each loan, so recording the dates for each financial company is the starting point.

2. Common conditions to check

Although the wording varies by source, three conditions commonly appear. First, check whether you owe debts to at least two financial companies that have joined the agreement. Second, check whether your total debt is KRW 1.5 billion or less (the guidance specifies KRW 1 billion in secured debt and KRW 500 million in unsecured debt). Third, check whether debts newly incurred within six months of the application date are approximately 30% of your remaining total debt. Because the use of “or more” and “or less” varies by source, check the original text before applying.

3. Read policy reform proposals as “planned”

The Financial Services Commission announced measures to improve the credit recovery support system for individual debtors in a policy briefing. The briefing includes rapid support for people at risk of delinquency before an account is 30 days past due, principal reductions for uncharged-off debt after 90 days past due, and special reductions for vulnerable groups who have difficulty making repayments. However, at the time this article was written, the implementation date and final conditions could not be confirmed in the CCRC’s notices. Therefore, read this as “reform has been announced,” not “it is already available.” Original policy briefing ↗

4. Checklist before applying

1) Debt list: Make a table listing the financial company, outstanding loan balance, date the account became overdue, and current number of days past due.

2) Changes in income: If there is a reason such as job loss, unpaid leave, business closure, or hospitalization due to illness, collect the dates and supporting documents.

3) Compare alternatives: Do not consider only debt adjustment; also receive comparative advice on the court procedures for individual rehabilitation and bankruptcy. This comparison is also included in official policy materials.

4) Confirm the application route: Immediately before applying, check the CCRC consultation telephone number and notices on its official website again. If a text message or call requests an account number or verification code during the application process, first confirm that it is an official channel.
