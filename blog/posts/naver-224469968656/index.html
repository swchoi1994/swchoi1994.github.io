When Windows keyboard input behaves strangely: How to turn off Sticky Keys and Filter Keys and a checklist

Seongwoo Choi · 2026.11.09 · 3 min read

If letters repeat, Sticky Keys pop-ups appear when you press Shift, or sounds play when you press lock keys, Windows accessibility keyboard features may be enabled and making input seem faulty.

In this article

  • 1. Sticky Keys: Enabled by pressing Shift five times
  • 2. Filter Keys: Enabled by holding the right Shift for 8 seconds
  • 3. Turn them off in Settings: Accessibility > Keyboard
  • 4. Distinguish Toggle Keys from device problems
  • 5. Checklist after turning them off

If a letter is entered multiple times after being pressed once, a Sticky Keys pop-up appears each time you press Shift, or sounds play when you press a lock key, Windows accessibility keyboard features may be enabled. These features are designed to process key combinations or brief input differently, so when enabled, they can make the keyboard seem faulty. This article organizes the steps for turning them off by cause. The shortcut list is based on Microsoft's official support documentation.

1. Sticky Keys: Enabled by pressing Shift five times

According to Microsoft's official list, pressing Shift five times turns Sticky Keys on or off. When Sticky Keys is enabled, modifier keys such as Ctrl, Shift, and Alt remain active after being pressed once, so shortcuts such as Ctrl+C or Ctrl+V may behave unexpectedly. If you often type capital letters and press Shift repeatedly, this pop-up may appear frequently.

2. Filter Keys: Enabled by holding the right Shift for 8 seconds

Holding the right Shift key for 8 seconds turns Filter Keys on or off. Filter Keys changes the keyboard to ignore brief or repeated keystrokes, so letters may be omitted when a key is pressed lightly, or it may feel as though you have to press the same key multiple times for it to register. If a pop-up appeared while you were holding the right Shift key, this feature is likely the cause.

3. Turn them off in Settings: Accessibility > Keyboard

In Windows 11, go to Settings > Accessibility > Keyboard and turn off Sticky Keys and Filter Keys. Turning off the shortcut options within each item as well can reduce repeated pop-ups when the same keys are pressed again. Menu names may vary depending on the Windows version and language settings, so look for the names displayed on your screen. After turning off the features, press Shift five times or hold the right Shift for 8 seconds to confirm that the pop-ups no longer appear.

4. Distinguish Toggle Keys from device problems

Holding Num Lock for 5 seconds turns on Toggle Keys, which may cause sounds when you press Caps Lock, Num Lock, or Scroll Lock. If the sounds are bothersome or you are unsure of the input state, also check the Toggle Keys settings in the same Accessibility menu. If the symptoms remain after turning off all accessibility features, turn on the On-Screen Keyboard with Win + Ctrl + O. If the on-screen keyboard works normally but the problem occurs only with the physical keyboard, you can suspect the keyboard connection or the device itself. In that case, compare it with another USB port or another keyboard.

5. Checklist after turning them off

1) After turning off Sticky Keys, do Shift-five-times pop-ups no longer appear? 2) Are Filter Keys and the right-Shift-for-8-seconds shortcut both turned off? 3) Have you confirmed whether the Num Lock sound is caused by Toggle Keys? 4) Have you compared the results from the on-screen keyboard and the physical keyboard?
