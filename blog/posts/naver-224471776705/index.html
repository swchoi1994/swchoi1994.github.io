Difference Between Parental Benefit and Child Allowance: Application Order for Children Aged 0–23 Months and Under 9

Seongwoo Choi · 2026.11.10 · 3 min read

Parental benefits and child allowances are often confused because both provide cash to families raising children. This guide compares eligibility, amounts, applications, and simultaneous receipt based on official guidance.

  • 1. Parental benefit: Children aged 0–23 months
  • 2. Child allowance: Children under age 9
  • 3. Can both be received during the same period?

Parental benefits and child allowances are often confused because both are programs that provide cash to families raising children. They can be easily distinguished by comparing the eligible ages, amounts, application methods, and whether both can be received at the same time. The information below is organized based on guidance from the Ministry of Health and Welfare, the Ministry of Gender Equality and Family, and Bokjiro. Actual eligibility for payment is determined after the application is reviewed.

1. Parental benefit: Children aged 0–23 months

The parental benefit is for families raising children aged 0–23 months. The monthly amount is 1,000,000 won for children under age 1 and 500,000 won for children aged 1. It can be received in cash or as a voucher. It was introduced in January 2023. Applications can be made in person at a community service center or through the Bokjiro website.

2. Child allowance: Children under age 9

The child allowance is for children under age 9. The amount is around 100,000 won per month and varies by region. For example, the amount is 100,000 won per month in the Seoul metropolitan area, 105,000 won outside the capital region, and 110,000 won in areas with declining populations. Starting in 2026, the eligible age was expanded to children under age 9; in 2027, it will expand to children under age 10, and in 2028, to children under age 11. Applications can also be made at a community service center or through Bokjiro.

3. Can both be received during the same period?

According to guidance from the Ministry of Health and Welfare and the Ministry of Gender Equality and Family, only one of the parental benefit, childcare fees, and full-day childcare service can be received at a time. Applications can be switched among these three programs. If the voucher amount is lower than the parental benefit, the difference is paid in cash. Since the child allowance is not included in this list, it is safest to directly confirm with the local community service center whether both can be received, based on the child’s age and the household’s circumstances.

4. Application deadline and retroactive payments

According to Bokjiro guidance, if you apply within 60 days of the date of birth, including the date of birth, payments are made retroactively from the month of birth. If you apply after 60 days, payments begin in the month of application. Therefore, it is advantageous to apply soon after childbirth. When one parent applies in person, the application can be made at any community service center nationwide; online applications are available only when the applicant is the child’s biological parent.

5. Checklist before applying

① Check the child’s age: children aged 0–23 months may qualify for the parental benefit, and children under age 9 may qualify for the child allowance. ② Check whether the programs overlap: remember that only one of the parental benefit, childcare fees, and childcare service can be received. ③ Contact the community service center at the address listed on the resident registration to confirm whether the child allowance can be received at the same time. ④ Apply within 60 days of birth. ⑤ Prepare the application documents and bank account information. The local community service center makes the final determination.
